Key Rules for Balancing

  • Law of Conservation of Mass: Total mass of reactants = total mass of products.
  • Subscripts vs. Coefficients: Change coefficients (numbers in front) to balance atoms; never change subscripts (small numbers in formulas).
  • Strategy: Count atoms on both sides, balance polyatomic ions as single units if unchanged, and save individual elements like O₂ or H₂ for last.

Core Notes · Topics 3 & 4

Target: Identify the limiting reactant to determine the maximum theoretical product yield and measure reaction efficiency.

Key action: Calculate the expected product yield from every starting reactant. The smaller output identifies the limiting reactant and sets the theoretical yield.

1. Limiting reactant and theoretical yield

Convert each reactant's starting mass to the same product. Use the balanced-equation coefficients and molar masses:

Yield from Reactant AYieldA = Mass A (g) × 1 mol AMA (g) × mol Productmol A × MProduct (g/mol)
Yield from Reactant BYieldB = Mass B (g) × 1 mol BMB (g) × mol Productmol B × MProduct (g/mol)

Rule: The smaller calculated product amount is the theoretical yield. The reactant that produces it is the limiting reactant.

2. Excess reactant remaining
Excess remaining (g) = Initial mass of excess (g) − Mass of excess consumed (g)
3. Percent yield
Percent yield = Actual yieldTheoretical yield × 100%

Actual yield is the amount of product measured in the lab; theoretical yield is the maximum product amount predicted from the limiting reactant.
